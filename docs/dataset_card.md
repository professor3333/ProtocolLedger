# Dataset card: cleaned WNUT-2020 Wet Lab Protocols

This card describes how ProtocolLedger obtains, converts, corrects and splits the
WNUT-2020 Wet Lab Protocol corpus. Every number below is regenerated by the scripts
listed in [Reproduction](#reproduction) and stored in the committed manifests under
`data/manifests/`.

## Source

| | |
|---|---|
| Corpus | WNUT-2020 Wet Lab Protocol corpus ([Tabassum, Xu and Ritter, 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.wnut-1.33/)) |
| Repository | [jeniyat/WNUT_2020_NER](https://github.com/jeniyat/WNUT_2020_NER), MIT License |
| Pinned commit | `9a02914e00f0a16c0c6820c4285dd72403a5a5a8` |
| Protocol text origin | [protocols.io](https://www.protocols.io); user content under [CC BY](https://www.protocols.io/terms) |
| Language | English |
| Format | BRAT standoff: one `.txt` per protocol (line 1 is the title, each later line is a step) and one `.ann` |

Only `train_data/`, `dev_data/` and `test_data_2020/` `Standoff_Format` directories are
used. The legacy `test_data/Standoff_Format` has entity annotations but no event or
relation records, and the other folder variants are not interchangeable.

**Attribution.** When redistributing protocol text, keep the corpus repository's notice
and credit the original protocols.io authors.

## Schema

18 entity types: Action, Amount, Concentration, Device, Generic-Measure, Location,
Measure-Type, Mention, Method, Modifier, Numerical, Reagent, Seal, Size, Speed,
Temperature, Time, pH.

14 directed relation types: Acts-on, Commands, Coreference-Link, Count, Measure,
Measure-Type-Link, Meronym, Mod-Link, Of-Type, Or, Product, Setting, Site, Using.

Event arguments (`E` records) become `action trigger → argument` relations; relation
records (`R`) become `Arg1 → Arg2`; endpoints that name an event resolve to the event's
action span. Repeated roles are normalized (`Acts-on2` → `Acts-on`).

## Pipeline

1. **Acquire** (`scripts/acquire.py`): download by commit, extract the selected
   directories read-only, verify the SHA-256 of all 1,209 files against
   `acquisition.json`.
2. **Convert** (`scripts/convert.py`): one canonical graph per protocol with the raw
   text verbatim and half-open `[start, end)` character offsets. Error-level problems fail
   conversion; warnings are kept and counted in `conversion_report.json`.
3. **Correct** (`scripts/build_splits.py`): apply the correction ledger
   `corrections.jsonl`.
4. **Deduplicate and split** (`scripts/build_splits.py`): resolve copies and revisions,
   write `dedup_report.json` and the frozen `splits.json`.

## Conversion findings

The converter reproduces the pre-project audit exactly: 603 documents, 88,164 entities
and 59,349 relations (event arguments plus relation records), including every per-label
count.

| Finding | Count | Handling |
|---|---:|---|
| Entity spans with leading/trailing whitespace (mostly U+00A0) | 291 (202 in test) | trimmed by the correction ledger |
| Action spans without an event record | 33 | kept |
| Identical `(head, label, tail)` relation records | 3 | merged; every origin kept |
| Ordered entity pairs with two relation labels | 2 | kept (multilabel) |
| Stored surface text differs from the offset slice (`test_data_2020/protocol_0667` T101) | 1 | offsets are authoritative |
| Discontinuous spans, dangling endpoints, unknown labels | 0 | would fail conversion |

## Correction ledger

`data/manifests/corrections.jsonl` lists every change made to gold annotations. Each
entry records the rule, document, record, and the before and after offsets and text.
Raw files are never modified. The ledger applies identically to all splits, including
test, and each cleaned document lists the corrections applied to it.

| Rule | Entries | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|
| `trim_edge_whitespace` | 291 | Boundary whitespace carries no meaning; leaving it in gold would penalize every system that predicts the clean span under strict boundary matching. |

## Deduplication

The released splits contain many copies and revisions of the same protocol, some across
the train/dev and dev/test boundaries. Exact matching on whitespace-normalized text finds
581 unique texts among 603 files. Re-segmented copies (the same words with different
line breaks or punctuation) and revisions are found with word-shingle similarity.

**Similarity.** Jaccard similarity of case-folded 5-word shingles, computed for all
pairs.

**Same protocol.** Two documents are the same protocol when
- their shingle Jaccard is at least **0.85**, or
- it is at least **0.5** and their normalized titles are equal.

Same-protocol links are merged into connected components.

**Keeper.** Each component keeps one document, in the most protected split it touches
(test > dev > train), then the lowest protocol number. The others are excluded. The test
split is never reduced, and no copy of a dev or test protocol remains in training.

**Template siblings.** Pairs at or above 0.5 that are not the same protocol are distinct
documents generated from a shared template, for example one vendor kit protocol adapted
per tissue or cell type. They are kept and recorded in `splits.json` so evaluation can
report them as a slice.

**How the thresholds were chosen.** Pairs at or above 0.85 were inspected individually.
Nearly all are copies or revisions that differ by re-segmentation, small edits, or an
edited title line. Two are close template variants inside train, which the threshold
merges:
- SYBR Gold staining "Case 1" vs "Case 2", at 0.87
- OmniPrep for blood vs cultured cells, at 0.86

Each costs one training document and cannot leak across splits. Between 0.5 and 0.85,
pairs with different titles were template siblings, for example OmniPrep for different
tissues, MojoSort kit variants, and NEB transformation protocols per strain. Pairs with
equal titles in that range were revisions.

| Exclusion | Documents |
|---|---:|
| Train copy of a dev protocol | 14 (2 exact, 12 near) |
| Dev copy of a test protocol | 1 (near) |
| Within-train duplicate | 42 (17 exact, 25 near) |
| Within-dev duplicate | 9 (3 exact, 6 near) |
| **Total excluded** | **66** |

Template siblings across splits remain for 13 dev and 3 test documents.

### Residual overlap (containment audit)

Jaccard stays low when a short document sits inside a much longer one. A later audit
(`scripts/leakage_audit.py`, [report](../reports/leakage/train-overlap.md)) therefore also
measures **containment**: the share of a document's 5-word shingles that occur in its
nearest training document. The splits were frozen before this audit and are not changed
by it.

| Documents | n | Containment ≥ 0.5 | Gold relations in those documents |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Dev | 112 | 20 | 1,586 of 10,065 (15.8%) |
| Test | 111 | 3 | 195 of 16,378 (1.2%) |
| Later-domain set (vs. the whole corpus) | 38 | 0 | — |
| Challenge set (vs. the whole corpus) | 11 | 0 | — |

Eight of these documents were not recorded as template siblings, because their Jaccard is
below 0.5:
- seven dev documents that are kit or strain variants of a longer training protocol, for
  example Q5 site-directed mutagenesis E0552 and E0554 vs. the kit quick protocol, Th9 vs.
  Th17 polarization, and CviRI vs. CviJI purification
- one test document, "Calculating burst size" (70 words), of which 84.5% occurs inside
  the training protocol "One-Step Growth Curves for Cyanophages" (774 words)

On dev, every system scores higher on documents that overlap with training (end-to-end
relation F1, document-level bootstrap 95% CIs):

| Containment | Documents | CRF + LR | SciBERT |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| < 0.1 | 72 | 0.546 [0.514, 0.578] | 0.617 [0.588, 0.649] |
| 0.1 to 0.5 | 20 | 0.612 [0.548, 0.679] | 0.662 [0.619, 0.716] |
| ≥ 0.5 | 20 | 0.709 [0.673, 0.745] | 0.714 [0.682, 0.745] |

So dev scores, and the model selection and calibration fitted on them, are somewhat
optimistic for unseen protocols. SciBERT's lead over CRF + LR is 7.1 points on
low-overlap documents and 0.5 points on high-overlap ones. The test split has almost no
such overlap, which is consistent with the lower test scores. The audit reads no test
predictions.

### Duplicate-copy agreement

Sixteen pairs of byte-identical texts were annotated separately in the release.
Comparing the two annotations gives a reference point for annotation consistency:

| | Matched | Copy A | Copy B | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Entities (label + exact span) | 1,276 | 1,474 | 1,482 | **0.863** |
| Relations (head span + label + tail span) | 712 | 999 | 1,005 | **0.711** |

Annotator identity is not recorded, so this is **duplicate-copy agreement**, not
inter-annotator agreement. It is computed on raw spans, before the correction ledger.

with duplicates.

## Limitations

- **Revisions.** Revisions with heavily rewritten text and different titles can fall
  below both thresholds. Template siblings share large amounts of text with training
  data, so scores on those documents may be optimistic.
- **Residual overlap.** 20 dev documents (15.8% of dev gold relations) have at least
  half their text in one training document, and score higher for every system. On test
  this affects 3 documents (1.2% of gold relations). See the containment audit above.
- **Domain.** All protocols come from one source (protocols.io) and a limited range of
  wet-lab subdomains. They were annotated in or before 2020.
- **Annotation consistency.** Duplicate-copy agreement shows meaningful inconsistency,
  especially for relations. This bounds what any model can score against a single gold
  copy.
- **Rare labels.** Some labels, such as Commands, Of-Type and pH, have a few hundred
  instances or fewer, so per-label conclusions for them are weak.
- **Implicit and conditional content.** Implicit arguments, negation and conditional
  execution are not systematically annotated.

## Reproduction

```bash
uv run python scripts/acquire.py       # pinned corpus → data/raw/ (verified)
uv run python scripts/convert.py       # → data/manifests/conversion_report.json
uv run python scripts/build_splits.py  # → corrections.jsonl, dedup_report.json, splits.json
```

Each step checks its input against the previous step's committed hashes, and each
committed manifest is byte-reproducible.

## New annotation sets (protocols.io, 2025–2026)

Two small sets test the model on text it was not built around: a **later-domain** set of
recent protocols (38 protocols, 301 steps) and a **challenge** set of steps chosen for
hard phenomena (11 protocols, 33 steps). The collection rules are in the
[README](../README.md#new-annotation-sets-protocolsio) and
`src/protocolledger/data/protocolsio.py`. Every decision is recorded in
`data/manifests/protocolsio_collection.json`.

- **Source and licence.** Public protocols.io protocols published 2025-09 to 2026-08,
  fetched through the official API with a developer token: 178 requests for the collection, one per second.
  Protocol text is protocols.io user content under CC BY (Terms of Service, section
  1(B)). Each source records its URL, DOI, authors, version and publication date. The
  API's own `license` field was empty for every collected protocol.
  The texts are committed in `data/annotation/` with that attribution; the repository's
  MIT license does not apply to them.
- **Independence.** No collected protocol matches a WNUT-2020 document, or another
  collected protocol, under the corpus dedup rule. The two sets share no protocol.
- **Splits.** Dev or test is assigned per whole protocol from a hash of its id, before
  annotation. The test portions are locked.
- **Text.** Each step is one line taken from the visible text of the protocols.io page.
  Notes, tables and figures are removed; other markup is not. The text keeps the authors'
  typing, including errors such as duplicated values (`~800~800 uL`), plain-text tables
  pasted into a step, and Unicode spaces.
- **Annotation.** No human gold has been written yet. The plan and tooling are for one
  annotator labelling each text from scratch with the corpus schema, without model
  suggestions; any consistency figure would be repeat-annotation consistency. The
  annotator has seen machine labels for six challenge dev protocols (pio-321801,
  pio-228894, pio-241598, pio-229673, pio-241749, pio-126304), so human labels for those
  would not be independent of them.
- **Machine labels.** In place of human gold, both sets carry machine-generated
  labels that no person has reviewed: later-domain has 3,409 entities and 2,354 relations,
  and challenge has 460 entities and 327 relations. They are stored in
  `machine_labels.jsonl` and built from the label specs in `machine_specs/`. They are
  kept apart from `gold.jsonl` and never become gold. Every score computed against them is
  reported as "vs. machine labels" and not as accuracy. They include the locked test portions
  (16 of 38 later-domain protocols, 5 of 11 challenge protocols). The declared releases
  were scored on those portions once, on 2026-10-15, with error counts but no example
  sentences; see the README results. Labels follow the train convention for each type
  pair: `build_machine_labels.py` lists any relation whose (head, tail) types occur fewer
  than 5 times in train. They also follow measured train span conventions: `Or` from the
  earlier mention to the later one (341 of 408 in train), "up to"/"within"/"approximately"
  outside quantity spans, "not" unlabelled (125 of 135), and title nouns such as
  "Preparation" as Methods (102 title words ending in "-tion" are Methods, one is an
  Action).
- **Challenge categories** are fixed text tests, not human judgements. A step tagged
  "implicit references" contains a pronoun or a phrase such as "the supernatant", which
  does not guarantee that the reference is implicit.
